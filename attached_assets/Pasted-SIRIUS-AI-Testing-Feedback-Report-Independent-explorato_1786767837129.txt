SIRIUS
AI Testing & Feedback Report

Independent exploratory benchmark - 18 prompt tests
14 August 2026
Overall finding
Sirius shows strong single-turn capability and can produce excellent answers, but reliability is uneven. The clearest development priorities are conversation state, constraint enforcement, final-answer consistency, search/tool routing, citation handling and maths rendering.

 
Prepared as practical product feedback for Lynn and Garry

1. Executive Summary
Sirius was tested across 18 prompts covering reasoning, hallucination resistance, finance, numerical analysis, instruction following, conversational context, live research, writing, logic, coding and multi-constraint problems. This was an exploratory QA exercise rather than a formal scientific benchmark, so the individual scores should be read as diagnostic indicators rather than league-table results.
The strongest overall impression is that the underlying single-prompt answer capability is promising. Sirius performed particularly well when the task was self-contained and clearly structured. Several answers were effectively benchmark-quality. The main weaknesses appeared around the application/orchestration layer and reliability: prior conversation context was not reliably available, constraints were sometimes ignored, answers could contradict their own calculations, and web search was sometimes triggered unnecessarily or despite an explicit instruction not to search.

3. What Sirius Does Well
• Strong single-turn reasoning on well-defined problems. Tests 1, 6, 8 and 18 were particularly good.
• Excellent instruction following when constraints are expressed clearly. Test 15 satisfied every requested formatting and content rule.
• Good hallucination resistance for unverifiable external claims. In Test 2, Sirius refused to fabricate details about a deliberately fictional paper.
• Good business writing. The email rewrite in Test 11 was concise, professional and natural without becoming overly corporate.
• Capable numerical and coding work. Sirius handled percentage reasoning well and produced sensible corrected Python implementations.
• Generally willing to acknowledge uncertainty rather than fabricate external facts, which is a valuable foundation for professional use.
4. Priority Findings
4.1 Conversation state / history - Critical
The most serious issue observed was failure to retain or receive prior turns reliably. After Sirius had answered an investment allocation question, a direct follow-up asking it to check that answer produced unrelated material about the meaning of 'verification'. A further prompt explicitly asking Sirius to recall the immediately preceding investment problem resulted in Sirius stating that no such prior exchange was visible.
For a conversational assistant this is a fundamental product issue. Users expect follow-ups such as 'make that shorter', 'check your previous answer' and 'use the figures I gave you earlier' to work without restating the original prompt.
4.2 Final-answer consistency - High
Several responses contained both correct reasoning and an incorrect assertion in the same answer. Examples included stating that free cash flow had increased exactly 40% before correctly explaining that free cash flow could not be calculated from the data supplied, and asserting that gross profit should be £9m before later confirming that the stated £4.8m gross profit was valid.
The issue is not simply lack of reasoning ability. Sirius often reaches the correct intermediate result but does not reliably reconcile every statement before presenting the final response.
4.3 Constraint enforcement / verification - High
Sirius sometimes repeats a constraint correctly and then violates it. In one investment test it allocated £60,000 to a project with an explicit £50,000 cap. In a logic test it listed assignments with Ben as the lawyer despite explicitly stating that Ben could not be the lawyer. In the scheduling test it found the correct solution, then incorrectly rejected 11:00 as being immediately before 12:00.
Test 18 is important counter-evidence: Sirius can solve a multi-constraint problem perfectly. The issue therefore appears to be reliability and final validation rather than an inability to perform constraint reasoning.
4.4 Search/tool routing - High
The 'LIVE INTELLIGENCE · SEARCHED IN REAL TIME' indicator appeared repeatedly on self-contained maths, logic, coding and summarisation prompts, including prompts explicitly stating 'Do not search the web'. Other prompts did not show the badge, suggesting search is not simply permanently enabled.
If the badge accurately reflects tool use, the routing logic should be reviewed to avoid unnecessary latency, cost and irrelevant retrieval context. If it does not accurately reflect tool use, the UI label itself should be corrected.
4.5 Live research depth and source attribution - Medium
Sirius correctly retrieved the current Bank of England Bank Rate and the date/rate of the last change, but did not locate the corresponding primary-source MPC minutes containing the requested vote split. This suggests the first retrieval step is useful but deeper follow-through could be improved.
Sirius also repeatedly referred to search results or primary sources as information 'you provided' when the user had not supplied them. This suggests retrieved documents may be represented to the model in a way that confuses source provenance.
4.6 Maths rendering / citations - Medium to Low
Raw LaTeX commands such as \\times, \\frac, \\text and \\ge were visible in multiple answers rather than being rendered as mathematics. Citation markers such as [1][2][5] were also not always presented in a way that made the underlying source obvious. These are comparatively straightforward presentation issues but materially affect perceived polish.
5. Recommended Development Priorities
Priority
Area
Recommendation
1
Conversation state/history
Ensure the model receives the intended prior turns and that follow-up prompts resolve against the active conversation.
2
Final-response reconciliation
Before output, check that the headline conclusion, calculations, explanation and final summary do not contradict each other.
3
Constraint validation
Run a deterministic or model-assisted final pass that tests every proposed result against every explicit constraint.
4
Search/tool routing
Search only when external/current information is needed or requested; honour explicit no-search instructions where appropriate.
5
Multi-step research
Allow retrieval to follow primary-source chains rather than stopping after the first relevant page.
6
Source provenance
Clearly distinguish user-supplied material from Sirius-retrieved material in the model context and UI.
7
Citation UX
Make citations clearly inspectable/clickable and expose source title, publisher and date where available.
8
Math rendering
Render LaTeX or convert mathematical expressions to clean user-facing notation before display.
6. Suggested Technical QA Checks
• Conversation-state regression: send a fact in turn 1, ask for an unrelated task in turn 2, then refer back to the fact in turns 3-10.
• Constraint validator: generate candidate output, parse each explicit rule, and independently mark each rule pass/fail before release.
• Contradiction scan: compare opening conclusion, calculations and closing conclusion for mutually incompatible claims.
• Search router tests: maintain a suite of prompts that must search, may search and must not search, and log the routing decision.
• Source-provenance tests: verify that Sirius says 'I found' or 'the retrieved source says' rather than 'you provided' when retrieval supplied the evidence.
• Research-depth tests: require a fact found only by following from an index/history page to the underlying primary document.
• Rendering tests: automated checks for leaked LaTeX tokens and unresolved citation markers in user-facing output.
7. Overall Assessment
Sirius is promising as an early-stage AI product. The testing does not suggest a fundamentally weak underlying model. On the contrary, several standalone tasks were answered at a very high level. The larger concern is variance: a professional user cannot rely on a system that produces a 10/10 answer on one structured task and then ignores an explicit constraint or loses the previous turn on the next.
The encouraging part is that many of the observed weaknesses look like product, orchestration, validation and presentation issues rather than a lack of core capability. Improving conversation state, tool routing and final-answer verification could therefore produce a substantial improvement without needing to redesign the entire product.
Bottom line: Sirius already demonstrates strong raw capability. The next development phase should focus less on adding features and more on making the existing intelligence consistently reliable, conversational and correctly orchestrated.
Appendix A - Scope & Caveats
This report is based on 18 manually executed prompts observed through the public Sirius interface on 14 August 2026. It is an exploratory product test, not a controlled benchmark against identical model versions, temperatures, system prompts, latency conditions or tool configurations on competing platforms. The scores are therefore intended to identify development priorities and reproducible behaviours rather than establish a definitive market ranking.
